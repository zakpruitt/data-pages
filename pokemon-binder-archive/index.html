Chapter I

How the archive works

The Michi Method mixes cards, art that spans pockets, and deliberate empty space instead of filling all nine slots. Treat each spread as a museum case: a few objects, lit well, with a label.

SafetyNothing sticky touches a card.

Adhesive goes on outer sleeves, acetate overlays, or the printed inserts. Every decoration can come off later.

HierarchyDecorate art pockets. Frame card pockets.

Cards get at most a label or a thin border on the outer sleeve. The heavy layering happens in the art pockets around them.

PaletteTwo colours, one metal, one motif.

Each spread picks from its Pokémon's dossier. Gold or silver, never both on one spread.

RestraintThree elements per pocket, max.

A sticker, a label, a holo shard. If you add a fourth, take one away.

SystemOne label system across the book.

Same fonts, same numbering, same position on every spread. That consistency is what makes it read as an archive.

AirLeave a black pocket on purpose.

An empty black slot next to a card does more than another sticker. Michi layouts rely on that negative space.

Sizes to design at

63 × 88 mm
Standard card. Cut every insert and overlay to this size so it slides in like a card.
66 × 91 mm
Typical sleeved size. Use for inserts going into an outer sleeve.
3 × 63 mm + 2 gutters
Full-page art. Measure the gap between pockets on your binder (often 4–7 mm), build the canvas at full width including gutters, then slice into nine tiles and throw away the gutter strips so the image lines up across pockets.
300 dpi
Export resolution. A 63 × 88 mm tile is about 744 × 1039 px.

Build order

  1. Pick the roster

    Choose the 10–20 Pokémon that get spreads. Open the Themes chapter for each one's palette and layout.

  2. Design inserts

    Canva or Affinity at the sizes above. Make the label set at the same time so typography matches.

  3. Dry-fit with no adhesive

    Load cards, inserts and overlays. Live with the layout for a few days before sticking anything.

  4. Decorate overlays and sleeves

    PET stickers and rub-ons go on acetate and outer sleeves, never the cards.

  5. Hide NFC tags

    Behind art inserts, one per spread. Program them last so the links are final.

  6. Build the audio cover

    Last, because it is the only step that is hard to undo. See the Audio chapter.

    Chapter VI

    The sounding cover

    All electronics live in the front cover, so card pages stay flat. Open the binder and the cover theme plays; press flat membrane buttons printed as ▶ MUSIC · CRY · EFFECT for the rest.

    Build ANo code

    Button soundboard

    DFPlayer Mini on its own. Battery, charger, slide switch, speaker, buttons.

    • Two buttons on IO1/IO2: short press = previous/next track, long press = volume.
    • Direct “play track N” keys through the ADKEY resistor ladder (0 Ω, 3 kΩ, 6.2 kΩ, 9.1 kΩ…). Check the DFPlayer datasheet table for which segment does what.
    • Cannot auto-play when the cover opens.
    • Fine as a first prototype on a breadboard.
    Build BRecommended

    Opens-and-plays cover

    Adds a thumb-sized Seeed XIAO board and a reed switch. About 20 lines of Arduino code.

    • Reed switch cuts all power while the binder is shut: zero standby drain.
    • Opening the cover powers up and plays the intro track.
    • Each membrane key plays a specific file.
    • Room to grow: per-section volume, random cries, a “now playing” LED.

    Wiring

    Where the magnet goes

    A reed switch only reacts within a centimetre or two, but the covers of a closed binder sit 4–5 cm apart. Two layouts that work:

    • Strap binder. Put the reed under the front cover where the elastic strap crosses it, and glue a magnet inside the strap. Strap on = silent. Slip it off = music. The Vault X Exo-Tec Strap binder suits this.
    • Flap or clasp. On a binder with a magnetic flap, mount the reed beside the existing clasp magnet.
    • No strap at all. Swap the reed for a light sensor on the XIAO and play when the inside cover sees light. Uses a little standby power.

    Thickness budget

    Lay parts side by side, never stacked. The deepest part sets the cavity depth. Approximate figures.

    Build the cavity from two layers of 5 mm foam board glued inside the cover, cut out around each part, and close it with a printed art panel.

    Per-page sound, plainly
    Detecting which page is open inside a 20-page binder isn't practical with switches. The cover handles one theme. For page-specific audio, use the NFC chapter: tap a page with your phone and its playlist opens.
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    Chapter VII
    Tap-to-open pages
    NFC stickers are paper-thin, need no battery, and give every spread its own sound or link. Hold a phone to the page and it opens whatever the tag points at.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    Setup
    
      
      
        
    1. Buy NTAG215 stickers, 25 mm round
      504 bytes is far more than a URL needs, and every modern phone reads them. Get a pack of 20+.
      2. 
        
    2. Install NFC Tools
      Free on iOS and Android. Choose Write → Add a record → URL.
      3. 
        
    3. Point at a link you control
      Write a short URL that redirects to the playlist or video. Then you can change the destination later without rewriting the tag.
      4. 
        
    4. Stick it behind an art insert
      On the back of the printed insert in an art pocket, never on a card. Mark the spot with a tiny symbol on the overlay so you know where to tap.
      5. 
        
    5. Test through the page
      iPhones read URL tags from the lock screen; Android needs the screen unlocked. Leave tags unlocked so you can rewrite them.

    What to link
    
        
      
          
    • Theme playlist. A Spotify or YouTube playlist per Pokémon. The dossiers list a starting track.
      • 
          
    • Pokédex entry. Bulbapedia or Serebii for the species.
      • 
          
    • The card itself. Its PriceCharting page, or a photo log of the pull.
      • 
          
    • Provenance. The video of the pack opening, or the con where you bought it.
      • 
          
    • Your own page. A personal page per spread with notes, artist credits and acquisition date. This data-pages site can host those.
      • 
        
    
      
    Metal kills the signal. Metallised holo film, foil card stock and the Pokémon cards' own holo layer can block a tag sitting directly behind them. Put tags behind plain paper pockets, or buy “anti-metal” NTAG215 tags for spreads heavy with foil.
    
      
    Mark the tap spot
    Use one consistent symbol on every overlay: a small gold ring, a PET “)))” sticker, or a museum-style “AUDIO GUIDE ◉” label. It reads like a gallery audio tour.
